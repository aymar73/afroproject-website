The Insight

Why the Euro Failed — and Why Africa Won't Repeat It

Greece and Germany joined the Euro for political reasons. They were not cointegrated — their economies did not move together. The result was a debt crisis that nearly destroyed the currency union.

Cointegration is the marriage license of currencies. Without it, monetary union is not integration — it is a handcuff.

Our Discovery

Using the Johansen Trace Test on 20 years of data across 54 African countries, we identified three statistically viable currency zones: West, East, and Southern Africa. Cross-zone cointegration does not exist.

We follow the math, not the map.

The Solution

Three Zones, One Destiny

Each zone issues its own currency, pegged to a shared Afro-Index. All three are 100% convertible at cointegration-derived rates. The index floats against USD/EUR — giving Africa a unified external voice.

The Governance

A Parliament of Central Banks

A single AI agent dictating policy to fifteen central banks would never be trusted. So we built a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework — one explainable KAN-based agent per central bank.

These agents communicate, negotiate, and learn cooperative policies that balance national sovereignty with zone-wide stability. Every decision is explainable. Every rule is enforced by smart contract. No human override.
