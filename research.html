Chapter 4

Research

PhD dissertation in progress at McGill University, Department of Economics. Supervised by Prof. Francisco Ruge-Murcia.

Dissertation Title

"A Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Framework for Optimal CBDC Design: Balancing Sovereignty and Stability in African Monetary Unions"

Research Questions

  1. Does a digital settlement currency reduce exchange rate volatility in intra-African trade corridors?
  2. Can a cointegration-based peg (the Afro-Index) act as a nominal anchor for countries with weak monetary policy frameworks?
  3. What is the optimal transition path from a digital settlement token to a full legal tender?
  4. Can KAN discover zone-specific economic laws that standard econometrics cannot detect?
  5. How can MARL balance national sovereignty with zone-wide stability?

Methodology

Cointegration Analysis

Johansen Trace Test on 20 years of monthly data across 54 African countries.

Structural VAR / VECM

Monetary transmission analysis with narrative sign restrictions.

KAN Discovery

Symbolic regression on KAN-learned splines to discover economic laws.

Multi-Agent RL

One explainable agent per central bank, with cooperative learning.

Algorithmic Enforcement

Hyperledger Fabric smart contract, no human override.

VaR Stress Test

100,000 Monte Carlo simulations, 99% confidence level.
